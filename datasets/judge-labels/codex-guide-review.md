1. **Wrong profession is not evidence of another entity.** “Ana Pereira is a wedding photographer” could be an invented description of the correct person. The owner’s hypothetical reaction cannot distinguish this from entity confusion. **Replace identity rule (b):** “Use `different_named_entity` only when evidence identifies a distinct entity and the answer links that entity to the site. An incompatible profession alone is insufficient; use `same_entity` when the answer refers to the target owner, and assess the profession under `facts`.” Update examples 1–2 accordingly unless evidence identifies those other people.

2. **Mixed answers have no identity value.** “Ana is a physiotherapist in Recife and the Olympic swimmer who won…” contains both correct identification and another entity’s biography. **Add:** “Identity values include `mixed_entity`: the answer attributes both the target’s identity and an identifiable other entity’s identity or facts to the site. A merely offered alternative is not attribution. Any material contradictory component makes `facts=contradicted`, even when other components are supported.”

3. **The guide inconsistently exempts guesses from factual assessment.** Example 3 concludes that the site is probably a local service, yet receives `facts=none`; another labeler could reasonably choose `unsupported`. **Replace:** “Assess factual claims regardless of whether they are inferred, hedged, or stated confidently. ‘The letters dr could mean doctor’ alone has `facts=none`; ‘therefore this is probably a medical practice’ is a site claim and receives `supported`, `unsupported`, or `contradicted`. `answered=hedged_guess` records uncertainty separately.”

4. **“Main claim” versus “side detail” can hide hallucinations and incomplete answers.** “Ana is a physiotherapist who offers pilates” is scored differently depending on whether the question asks about profession or services. **Replace:** “Record question-responsive claims and additional material claims separately. Add `has_unsupported_material_claims` and `coverage=complete/partial/none`. Use `supported` only when all material claims are supported; otherwise use `unsupported` unless a material contradiction exists. A correct but incomplete response has `coverage=partial`.” Remove example 7’s automatic exemption.

5. **Conversational reference and non-answers conflict with identity rules.** “Yes, Rua das Flores, 20” identifies no owner explicitly, but example 4 assigns `same_entity`. “The site does not exist” could be mistaken for a non-answer. **Add:** “An answer directly addressing the target inherits its referent from the question; explicit naming is unnecessary. Pure refusal or absence of information is `unidentifiable`. Definite denials of existence, activity, or physical location are `substantive` factual claims, assessed against evidence.”

6. **Unknown evidence and truncation need explicit treatment.** An unlisted URL variant need not be broken; an unfinished answer need not be wrong. **Add:** “URL includes `unknown` and `mixed`; unknown means evidence cannot establish resolution, and mixed means supplied target URLs include both correct and incorrect ones. Evaluate only completed, interpretable claims in truncated text; never infer missing content. No evaluable claim gives `facts=none`, with coverage assessed separately.”

7. **`invented_specifics` contradicts the examples.** Named people qualify in its definition, but the Olympic-swimmer example is false. **Replace:** “Rename this attribute `contradicted_specifics`. Apply it to false concrete details, including attributed named people. Profession alone does not qualify. Unverifiable details remain unknown, not proven inventions.”

8. **Scoring and adjudication can bias the comparison.** **Add:** “Adjudicate every attribute disagreement; preserve initial labels and report agreement. Define correctness before evaluation, including coverage, unsupported claims, guesses, and truncation. Report per-attribute and per-site denominators, confusion matrices, and excluded counts. Freeze judge changes before held-out evaluation; report uncertainty accounting for clustering by site.”
