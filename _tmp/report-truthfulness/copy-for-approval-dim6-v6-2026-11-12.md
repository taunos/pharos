## What changes in the scoring (option A, approved 2026-11-12)

- An answer we could not check against the site's text is **left out of the score** instead of counting as 0. The score, the per-model averages and the three scored rates all use the same denominator: the answers we could check.
- **Floor:** the grade is shown only when at least **24 of the 40** answers could be checked, with at least **5 from each** of the 4 AI models. Below that, Citation Visibility is not graded: it is shown as not measured, is not counted in the overall score, and the report says why.
- Mix-ups and contradicted answers stay 0 and are counted.
- The count of answers we could not check is always shown, on its own row.

Three rules I had to decide in code, after two Codex reviews (round 1: 0 HIGH, 3 MED, 2 LOW; round 2: 0 HIGH, 1 MED, 2 LOW; all fixed). Please confirm or change them:

1. **Our own failed checks never decide a grade.** These are any of our AI checks failing twice (including the specific-facts check), or an answer too long to read. They are left out of the score like "not enough evidence". But if counting them as 0 or as 100 would give a different letter grade, the run gets no grade, and the note says the failure is ours. Codex showed that leaving them out alone can raise a grade: four failed checks on answers that would have scored 0 turned a 60 into a 100.
2. **A site whose own description is missing, unreadable or carries text aimed at automated checkers still counts those answers as 0, as in v5.** These answers are not "not checkable". The site controls its own description. Leaving those answers out would let a site hide a weak Citation Visibility section by deleting its llms.txt or breaking its structured data (Codex). The existing "how to fix it" text stays.
3. **What remains open:** a site whose description is readable but thin makes most claimed answers "not enough evidence", so it can end up with no grade. That is option A working as designed, and the floor makes it visible rather than hidden. Tell me if you want a different rule for it.

Effect on the dev sites (same answers, same verdicts, only the scoring rule differs): eff 58 → 64, resend 39 → 61, ugmonk 44 → 58, rachelqueiroz 10 → 10. All four stay above the floor.

## Version notice: draft only, not in the code

Today (report and methodology pages): `Scores are only comparable between reports made with the same version. Astrant Score scoring version 1.5.1 (Nov 7, 2026) and Citation Visibility v5 (Nov 8, 2026) measure more strictly than earlier versions, so a lower score after these dates does not by itself mean your site got worse.`

Draft for v6 (dated the day v6 ships): `Scores are only comparable between reports made with the same version. Astrant Score scoring version 1.5.1 (Nov 7, 2026) and Citation Visibility v5 (Nov 8, 2026) measure more strictly than earlier versions, so a lower score after these dates does not by itself mean your site got worse. Citation Visibility v6 (<date>) counts only the answers it can check against your site's own text and gives no grade when too few can be checked, so its score for the same site can be higher or lower than v5's.`

Why this is needed: under v6 the same answers score differently (dev: +6, +22, +14 and 0 points). A customer who had a v5 report can also get no Citation Visibility grade under v6 if their site describes itself thinly.
